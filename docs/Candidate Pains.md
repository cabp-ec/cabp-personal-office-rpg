## Overview

Nowadays job hunting is challenging enough. Sadly, it's not always clear on the side of hiring organizations (HO) why customization makes it even more. We'll explain here exactly why.

We can group pains like so

## Keyword Matching

**The skimming**

Recruiters average a mere _6 seconds skimming static resumes_. This rapid pacing causes cognitive fatigue, forcing the brain to rely on lazy, biased mental shortcuts (e.g., rejecting an applicant simply because of an unfamiliar school or non-linear career, a.k.a. _"gap"_).

ATS systems/platforms accelerate the _"screening"_ process, in many cases by matching keywords between the candidate's resume and the job description. Unfortunately, the _6 seconds skimming_ seems to be moved from a document to the ATS.

> From _~6 seconds skimming a candidate's resume_ to _~2 seconds looking at the candidate's resume word-match score_.


**A tide rises all boats**

As a result of automated keyword-matching to discriminate candidates, they started to use automated ways to match the _"keywords"_ from a job description. Many recruiters and hiring organizations don't like when this natural balance occurs.

What is worst, this has led to the generation of _actual_ fake candidates as much as the misperception of _"fake candidates"_: labelling a candidate as _fake_ based on conscious bias (like the long dash, certain words or patterns that only the recruiter knows).


**Painful from the title**

Many job offerings or roles are prefixed or suffixed with "agentic" or "AI", implying the use of AI as requirement.

Furthermore, you can get (within the same day) an alert for "Senior QA Automation Engineer" and "Senior QA Automation Engineer - AI Testing" from the same company. Maybe even both posts refer to the same position/role, we cannot know unless there's transparency in the process.

AI (and its dangers) are already here and wont go anywhere, we must accept that. If we do, all this ai prefixing/suffixing habit becomes futile. As long as this habit persist, candidates must customize each resume to match whatever the JD says the role is.


**Increasing the pain**

As we all know, each job description is different, although in most cases the job is pretty much the same. This increases the exhaustion of customization even further, because one organization could use entire words and others an acronym, or even use different wording for the same meaning (e.g. _"fullstack"_ vs _"full-stack"_).

## A long story short is hard

The longer your experience the more challenging the customization is, because you need to ensure to include the relevant _"information"_ (i.e. keywords) without showing "gaps" in as less pages as possible (some "professional resume writers" recommend just one).

> Focusing only on specific sections of a long experience automatically generates the false perception of a _"gap"_.

---
## The Paywall

As if having to do all that is not challenging enough, resume-customization platforms like Jobscan allow candidates a specific amount of attempts to check if the candidate's resume has the minimum _"score"_.

When a candidate needs to increase the _"score"_, the allowed amount is never enough, because when they apply they may use the same document 2 or 3 times for the same role, then repeat the process to apply to 2 or 3 different job offerings, making the need exponential, quickly overpassing the limit.

---
## Global Competition

In this digital era, globalization for job hunting has leave a mark: intense global competition. We can see this effect when recruiters and HO report literally thousands of applicants in just a few days.

Additionally, every time that a major layoff happens, every candidate could easily face thousands of competitors, sometimes from one day to the next.

---
## Fake Jobs, Scammers and Stagnant Processes

We've all been there. What's worst, we see this happening massively. In many cases, candidates customize a resume just to end-up in a new company's "candidate pool".

We all have seen (and suffered) the burden of tailoring complex applications for fake or stagnant postings that employers have no intention of filling. However, we also consider the fact that there are hiring organizations which are just too slow (a red flag for us).
