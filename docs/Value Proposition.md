## Overview

In far gone times, we used to create our resume once, get multiple copies and visit companies (which we usually found on the newspaper) to _"drop"_ a literal photocopy of the resume; then call a couple of days later to know how the process is going. And what was cool, is that most of them did tell how it's going.

Technologies evolve, then we used to send a copy via e-mail, then we participate in multiple online boards looking for real, active career opportunities, phone calls have become scarce (if any).

> Job search is nowadays quite more challenging. In fact, _"job search"_ is gone; now we all do _"job hunting"_.

## For the Unemployed
### 1. Standardization Over Customization

The advent of Application Tracking Systems (ATS) has risen the need to customize the resume and/or cover letter for each specific role. As logical as that might seem, there are factors (like fake jobs and candidates) which —in conjunction with customization, have transformed job hunting into more than a job per-se.

**Application Exhaustion** is the most direct of [[Candidate Pains]] related to customization, for which we provide the following pain relievers.

#### An Enhanced XYZ

By providing the candidate with methods to input his/her _full professional and educational history_ as a _"experiences dataset"_, **ONCE**. To that effect, we apply the _"XYZ formula"_ popularized by Laszlo Bock, Google's former Senior Vice President of People Operations.

> We _"enhance"_ the XYZ formula by appending what we call _"professional metadata"_ to it. This allows the candidate or the recruiter to filter _resume bullet points_ relevant for one role or another, without needs of customization.

#### An Standardized Curriculum

We promote an **_standardized curriculum vitae_** for candidates. That's right, the whole CV, this is important for job hunting purposes and automation alike.

> By applying a structured writing framework in the gamification of both job hunting and hiring, we immediately standardize the resume creation and the reading experiences.

#### Zero Keyword Matching

We don't match candidates with keywords, in fact, we don't match candidates with recruiters or HOs at all, that happens by means of mutual interaction in the RPG experience.

> By leveraging mutual interaction between recruiters and candidates, we make the _"signs of interest"_ from each side transparent and measurable.

#### Disrupting the X-Second Review Bias

Because the data is delivered through structured game loops based on a pre-defined _"hero journey"_, recruiters focus on core competencies and verified skills rather than surface-level heuristics (~2 ~6 seconds skimming).

> By transforming the review process into an interactive gameplay mechanic, we slow down the reader and increase cognitive engagement.

### 2. Scoring Hiring Organizations

The use of keyword-matching to filter candidates has increased the use of a score based on it. Many organizations use 70% or 80% as a minimum score for a candidate to be considered. We're turning this all the way round.

Every HO starts with a 100% score, it gets reduced depending on candidate's reports, audits and our own research; let's face it, we all have identified scammers (individuals and organizations) and new staff-augmentation companies looking to create their "candidate pool".

#### Zero Discrimination

**Discrimination** is one of [[Candidate Pains]] we relieve by applying an score to the HO rather than to the candidate.

Certainly, we cannot manage nor be responsible for 3rd parties' decisions; this reinforces the need for transparency from posting a job offer all the way until it's' closed.

Reasons to reduce the HO score:

- Labelling a candidate as _"overqualified"_.
- Rejecting a candidate due to age preferences, country of residence, nationality, ethnicity, religion, country of residence, nationality

Automatic promotion:

- `[TBC]` Candidates labelled as _"overqualified"_ automatically get some kind of promotion, because, if anything, _"overqualified"_ should be considered a sign of experience.

### 3. Screening Hiring Organizations

HOs are screened (at the AUF lobby) when they join, they must accept to share public information and transparency along the whole candidate search and hiring process.

To that effect, they must disclose:

- How long the search will take: this is the sum of _candidate search_, _screening_, _interviews_ and _final decision_.
- The total number of interviews for every job offer.
- Tryouts are required: yes/no, how many and difficulty level for each one.
- Building a candidate pool: yes/no.

Additionally, they must accept:

- Audits for stagnant processes (they'll get a visit from the _Plumber_).
- To follow the established procedures for candidate pools.

Reasons to lower the HO score:

- We recommend a search and hiring season of 1 month, if the HO uses more, they score is reduced, the longer the season the bigger the reduction.
- Not respecting the disclosed information.

### 4. Professional Career Paths

We partner with [The Odin Project](https://www.theodinproject.com/about) to provide certified career paths for candidates. By doing this, we're able to help the open-source community in the generation of real, useful content, which increases the chances of being discovered by a HO or a new customer.

> There's a difference between knowing the path and walking the path.

A career path, by definition, requires progression. A path, by nature, will have its "ups" and "downs". We consider that an opportunity.

Career paths generated in companion of The Odin Project and published in our platform will have a predefined progression as part of the _"candidate's journey"_; which in turn allows us to define well-crafted, short-enough and rational, community-vetted _"tryouts"_ designed for the candidate to test himself.

> The _"Certified by Odin"_ experience.

When candidates progress in their path and successfully overpass tryouts, they get an official certification by The Odin Project (hence the name of the experience) and us, with the proper endorsement of our sponsors.

### 6. Better Interaction

In this digital era, we cannot guarantee human interaction between the recruiter/hiring organization and candidates. Hence the need for transparency in the hiring process, but also from candidates.

As a matter of fact, the gamified experience as a whole is a better experience than just sending a pdf into a black hole (or a blue one...), because it has a structured and standardized interaction for _creating_ and _"reading"_ the candidate's resume.

#### Ghosting

Without a doubt, this is a challenging pain to relieve, simply because we have no control over it. This is reinforced if we consider the following:

1. It's just impossible to reply individually to literally thousands of candidates.
2. Let's face it: for most organizations employees and contractors are just another asset, sad but true.

> We transform ghosting into networking!

Let's dive into this in a logical manner.

**1. Candidate Queue**

The gamified experience for both candidates and recruiters/HOs guarantees that only a few candidates are considered.

The more interaction exists between candidates and recruiters/HOs the shorter the hiring season should be.

If the hiring season is short, candidates should be hired quickly. If candidates are hired faster, there's not much need for them to keep _"playing"_ in the platform, except for career paths.

> Inadvertently, we create a in-batch candidate queue mechanism.

**3. Group Reply**

During initial screening, HOs must disclose the amount of candidates they are willing to reply to after the hiring season. A value of zero is possible, but will reduce the score of the HO.

`[TBC]` After the hiring season, recruiters/HOs **MUST** reply to the specified amount of candidates, which are selected by them, and they **MUST** do it **ONCE** for all (e.g. one single loom video naming 5 candidates that nearly make it or one single e-mail for all). This, at least, allows candidates to have the chance to contact each other. We expect HOs to respect this rule, we also expect candidates to respect HOs by not reporting false claims of HOs breaking this rule.

If there are fewer candidates participating in the hiring season, the list of remaining ones who didn't make it and didn't get a group-reply should be relatively short.

**4. Constructive Closure**

`[HOs should ♥ this]` If the HO used our career paths and/or tryouts, then we'll have actionable data to automatically elaborate a **constructive feedback** at the end of the hiring season, personalized for every single not-hired candidate; regardless if they were contacted by the HO or not.

**5. Data Reusability**

`Gain creator` When a candidate is _ghosted_, his/her resume is **automatically delivered to another company** looking for candidates with the same or similar professional profile. The candidate's profile is also shared (automatically) with public and private initiatives within our community and partners'.

> This enables networking not only with recruiters/HOs but also with well-stablished alternative initiatives.

#### Identity Promotion & Entrepreneurship

**Identity Erosion** is one of the [[Candidate Pains]] that we intent to relieve. Instead of let erosion just happen, we take it as an opportunity for quite the opposite.

- The fact that this is an open-source initiative, already helps to promote the identity of unemployed participants.
- `[Gain creator, VCs should ♥ this]` Members of our community are able to create open-source projects, just like we're doing now; but also closed-source and for-profit private endeavors (including our own).

### 7. Community-Driven

**Vetted Processes**

As an open-source and free-to-use initiative, all our processes and policies are vetted by the community and our sponsors before being implemented.

Additionally, our community helps us to identify fake companies and jobs, as well as scammers and illegal/unethical actions.


**Transparency & Traceability**

_We don't hide visitors_. Whoever joins our platform has a public profile, we'll never implement features to hide companies or recruiters who visit a candidate's _little office_. 

This allows us to make the interaction transparent, by letting candidates know _exactly_ who is or has seen his/her profile every time (activity logs) or has shown a more deep interest by signing the _guest book_.

It's a well known fact that a simple job post can receive thousands of applicants in a few days. In our platform, everybody is free to see the TOTAL number of candidates that applied for a job, not just 100. Additionally, they can see details about how the hiring process is _flowing_ for him/her or any other candidate that has applied for the same job.

By applying a structured narrative into gamification ensures that the interaction between a candidate and a recruiter/HO is real; it allow enables us to implement a better tracking mechanism by using dialogue options to that effect.

## Business of Platform?

- We're connecting 2 major disparate segments: real people and real job posters.




The narrative architecture rejects sterile corporate structures, implementing Christopher Vogler's screenwriting framework, _**The Writer's Journey**_. Within this system:

- **The Recruiter is the Hero:** They are pulled from their "Ordinary World" of generic text-based candidate filtering into a "Special World" (a digital twin/tiled map representation of your physical home office).
- **The Candidate is the Mentor/Herald:** By interacting with objects in the office (e.g., clicking on a computer or a bookshelf), the player unlocks narrative milestones where the candidate provides contextual guidance, professional insight, and personal values.
